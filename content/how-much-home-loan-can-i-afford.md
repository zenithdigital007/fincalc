# How Much Home Loan Can You Afford?

Taking a home loan is the biggest financial commitment most Indian families will ever make. Getting the amount right matters — too little and you can't buy what you want; too much and you'll be under financial stress for decades.

Here's a practical, step-by-step guide to figuring out your ideal home loan amount.

## The Golden Rule: 40% EMI-to-Income Ratio

Most banks in India use this rule:

**Your total EMI obligations should not exceed 40–50% of your gross monthly income.**

This includes all existing EMIs (car loan, personal loan, credit card payments) plus the new home loan EMI.

### Example

Monthly income: ₹1,00,000
Max allowed total EMI (40%): ₹40,000
Existing EMIs (car loan): ₹8,000
**Available for home loan EMI**: ₹40,000 − ₹8,000 = **₹32,000**

At 8.5% for 20 years, ₹32,000/month can support a loan of approximately **₹36–37 lakh**.

## The 3x / 5x Income Rule

A simpler rule of thumb:
- Conservative: Home loan ≤ 3× annual income
- Standard: Home loan ≤ 5× annual income
- Aggressive: Home loan ≤ 6× annual income (only if income is expected to grow significantly)

| Annual Income | Conservative Loan (3x) | Standard Loan (5x) |
|--------------|------------------------|---------------------|
| ₹6,00,000    | ₹18,00,000             | ₹30,00,000          |
| ₹12,00,000   | ₹36,00,000             | ₹60,00,000          |
| ₹20,00,000   | ₹60,00,000             | ₹1,00,00,000        |

## Factors That Determine Your Home Loan Eligibility

### 1. Monthly Income
Higher income = higher loan eligibility. Banks consider basic salary, allowances, and sometimes rental income.

### 2. Credit Score
- **750+**: Best rates, highest eligibility
- **700–749**: Good eligibility, slightly higher rate
- **650–699**: Limited options, higher rate
- **Below 650**: Loan may be rejected

### 3. Age
- Younger borrowers (25–35) get longer tenures (up to 30 years), reducing EMI
- Older borrowers (50+) may only get 10–15 year tenures, making EMI higher

### 4. Existing Liabilities
Every existing EMI reduces how much you can borrow for a home loan.

### 5. Employment Type
- Salaried employees of large companies: Highest eligibility
- Self-employed professionals: Usually 80–90% of salaried eligibility
- Business owners: May need 2–3 years of ITR history

## What Costs Beyond EMI?

Many first-time buyers focus only on EMI but forget these costs:

| Cost | Typical Amount |
|------|---------------|
| Down payment (20%) | ₹10L for a ₹50L home |
| Registration + Stamp Duty | 5–8% of property value |
| Home loan processing fee | ₹5,000–₹25,000 |
| Property insurance | ₹3,000–₹8,000/year |
| Maintenance charges | ₹2,000–₹5,000/month |
| Interior/furnishing | ₹3L–₹15L |

**Plan for these upfront costs separately from your EMI budget.**

## How to Increase Your Home Loan Eligibility

1. **Add a co-applicant** (spouse or parent) — this can increase eligibility by 30–50%
2. **Clear existing loans** before applying
3. **Improve credit score** by paying EMIs on time for 6+ months
4. **Opt for a longer tenure** — this reduces EMI, allowing a higher loan
5. **Show all income sources** — rental income, freelance income, interest income

## Practical Examples

### Case 1: Young couple, combined income ₹1.5L/month
- Available for home EMI: ₹55,000 (40% of ₹1.5L minus ₹5K other EMIs)
- Loan at 8.5% for 25 years: **≈₹62–65 lakh**
- With 20% down: Can afford a home worth **₹75–80 lakh**

### Case 2: Single earner, income ₹80,000/month
- Available for home EMI: ₹28,000 (40% of ₹80K minus ₹4K other EMIs)
- Loan at 8.5% for 20 years: **≈₹30 lakh**
- With 20% down: Can afford a home worth **₹37.5 lakh**

## The Bottom Line

Don't max out your eligibility just because you can. A good rule: your **home loan EMI should ideally be 25–30% of take-home salary**, not 40–50%. The extra buffer protects you during job changes, medical emergencies, or interest rate hikes.

**Our recommendation**: Borrow 20–25% less than your maximum eligibility. Use the savings for investments, emergency fund, and prepayments.
