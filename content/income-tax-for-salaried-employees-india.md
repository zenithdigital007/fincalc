# Income Tax for Salaried Employees in India: New vs Old Regime (FY 2025-26)

Income tax is one of the most confusing aspects of personal finance for salaried employees in India — especially since the government introduced the new tax regime and has been tweaking the rules every year. This guide cuts through the confusion and gives you a clear, practical understanding of how your salary gets taxed in FY 2025-26.

---

## The Two Tax Regimes in India

Since FY 2020-21, Indian taxpayers have a choice between two regimes:

### New Tax Regime (Default from FY 2023-24)
- **Lower tax rates** — see slab table below
- **Simpler** — very few deductions and exemptions allowed
- **Recommended for**: People with fewer deductions (young earners, those without home loans or heavy 80C investments)

### Old Tax Regime (Optional — must be chosen at filing)
- **Higher headline rates** but allows **many deductions and exemptions**
- Can significantly reduce tax if you have: home loan, HRA, 80C investments, NPS, medical insurance
- **Recommended for**: Salaried people with home loans, HRA, and systematic 80C/80D investments

---

## FY 2025-26 New Tax Regime — Revised Slabs

The Union Budget 2025 further sweetened the new regime. The key headline change: **no tax up to ₹12 lakh** (after the ₹75,000 standard deduction, effective zero tax up to ₹12.75 lakh for salaried).

| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
|-------------|----------|
| Up to ₹4,00,000 | NIL |
| ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 | 10% |
| ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000 | 15% |
| ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000 | 20% |
| ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above ₹24,00,000 | 30% |

**Standard deduction under new regime**: ₹75,000 (from FY 2024-25 onwards)  
**Rebate under Section 87A**: Full tax rebate if income ≤ ₹12,00,000 (making effective zero tax up to ₹12,75,000 after standard deduction)

**Health and Education Cess**: 4% on the calculated tax

### Quick Tax Calculation Examples (New Regime)

**Example 1 — Annual CTC ₹10 lakh (Gross ₹10L, net taxable ₹9.25L after ₹75K standard deduction)**
- Up to ₹4L: ₹0
- ₹4L–₹8L: 5% of ₹4L = ₹20,000
- ₹8L–₹9.25L: 10% of ₹1.25L = ₹12,500
- Total before cess: ₹32,500
- Since ₹9.25L ≤ ₹12L, **rebate under 87A applies → Tax = ₹0**
- **Effective tax = ₹0**

**Example 2 — Annual gross ₹18 lakh (taxable ₹17.25L after standard deduction)**
- Up to ₹4L: ₹0
- ₹4L–₹8L: ₹20,000
- ₹8L–₹12L: ₹40,000
- ₹12L–₹16L: 15% of ₹4L = ₹60,000
- ₹16L–₹17.25L: 20% of ₹1.25L = ₹25,000
- Total: ₹1,45,000 + 4% cess = **₹1,50,800**

**Example 3 — Annual gross ₹30 lakh**
- Up to ₹4L: ₹0
- ₹4L–₹8L: ₹20,000
- ₹8L–₹12L: ₹40,000
- ₹12L–₹16L: ₹60,000
- ₹16L–₹20L: ₹80,000
- ₹20L–₹24L: ₹1,00,000
- ₹24L–₹29.25L (after ₹75K standard deduction): 30% of ₹5.25L = ₹1,57,500
- Total: ₹4,57,500 + 4% cess = **₹4,75,800**

## Old Tax Regime — Slabs and Key Deductions

| Income Slab | Tax Rate |
|-------------|----------|
| Up to ₹2,50,000 | NIL |
| ₹2,50,001 – ₹5,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000 | 20% |
| Above ₹10,00,000 | 30% |

The old regime looks worse on paper, but the deductions can dramatically reduce taxable income.

### Key Deductions Under the Old Regime

**Section 80C — Up to ₹1,50,000 deduction**
Eligible investments and expenses:
- EPF (Employee Provident Fund) — automatically deducted from salary
- PPF contributions
- ELSS (Tax-saving mutual funds) — 3-year lock-in, market-linked
- Life insurance premium
- NSC (National Savings Certificate)
- 5-year tax-saving FD
- Principal repayment on home loan
- Children's school tuition fees

**Section 80D — Medical Insurance Premium**
- Up to ₹25,000 for self + family
- Additional ₹25,000 for parents (₹50,000 if parents are senior citizens)
- Total: Up to ₹75,000 deduction

**HRA (House Rent Allowance) Exemption**
If you receive HRA as part of your salary and pay rent, you can claim exemption. The exemption is the **minimum** of:
1. Actual HRA received
2. 50% of basic salary (metro cities) or 40% (non-metro)
3. Actual rent paid minus 10% of basic salary

**Example**: Basic salary ₹50,000/month, HRA received ₹20,000/month, actual rent paid ₹18,000/month (Mumbai)
1. HRA received = ₹20,000
2. 50% of basic = ₹25,000
3. Rent − 10% of basic = ₹18,000 − ₹5,000 = ₹13,000
→ Exemption = **₹13,000/month** = ₹1,56,000 annually

**Section 80CCD(1B) — NPS Contribution**
Additional ₹50,000 deduction for contributions to National Pension System — over and above 80C limit.

**Section 24(b) — Home Loan Interest**
Up to ₹2,00,000 deduction on interest paid on home loan for self-occupied property.

**Leave Travel Allowance (LTA)**
Travel expenses for domestic journeys (twice in 4-year block) are exempt from tax.

**Standard Deduction (Old Regime)**: ₹50,000

## New vs Old Regime: Which is Better for You?

The break-even point depends on your deductions. Here's a practical comparison at ₹18 lakh gross income:

| | New Regime | Old Regime |
|--|------------|------------|
| Gross Income | ₹18,00,000 | ₹18,00,000 |
| Standard Deduction | ₹75,000 | ₹50,000 |
| 80C investments | Not allowed | ₹1,50,000 |
| Home Loan Interest (24b) | Not allowed | ₹2,00,000 |
| NPS 80CCD(1B) | Not allowed | ₹50,000 |
| 80D (health insurance) | Not allowed | ₹25,000 |
| **Taxable Income** | **₹17,25,000** | **₹13,25,000** |
| **Tax (approx.)** | **₹1,50,800** | **₹81,000** |

At ₹18 lakh income with a home loan and good 80C investments, the **old regime saves ₹70,000** in this example.

### General Rule of Thumb

**Stick with New Regime if:**
- Income below ₹7.75 lakh (zero tax anyway under 87A)
- No home loan, no HRA, minimal 80C deductions
- You find the old regime too complicated
- Your total deductions are under ₹3–3.5 lakh

**Consider Old Regime if:**
- You have an active home loan (₹2L interest deduction + 80C principal)
- You pay significant rent and get HRA
- You maximise 80C (EPF + PPF + ELSS) + 80D + NPS
- Total deductions exceed ₹4–5 lakh

## Understanding Your Salary Slip Components

For salaried employees, tax is deducted monthly by the employer as **TDS (Tax Deducted at Source)**. Here are the key components:

| Component | Taxable? | Notes |
|-----------|----------|-------|
| Basic Salary | Yes | Typically 40–50% of CTC |
| HRA | Partially | Exempt if you pay rent (see formula above) |
| Special Allowance | Yes | Fully taxable |
| LTA | Partially | Exempt for travel, twice in 4 years |
| Meal Coupons | Partially | ₹50/meal exempt (Sodexo etc.) |
| EPF Employer Contribution | No (up to limit) | Exempt up to 12% of basic |
| Gratuity | Partially | Exempt up to ₹20 lakh |
| Bonus / Incentive | Yes | Fully taxable |
| ESOP (on exercise) | Yes | Taxed as perquisite |

## Submitting Investment Declarations (Form 12BB)

At the start of each financial year, your employer asks you to submit **Form 12BB** — an investment declaration stating what 80C, 80D, HRA, and other deductions you plan to claim.

**Important**: This declaration is just an estimate. You must submit actual proof (investment receipts, rent receipts) to your employer by **January 31** of the financial year. Failure to submit proofs means the employer will deduct higher TDS in the last 2 months of the year.

---

## Filing Your ITR (Income Tax Return)

Even if your employer deducted the correct TDS, **filing your ITR is mandatory** if your gross income exceeds ₹2.5 lakh. The due date is typically **July 31** for salaried individuals (without audit requirement).

Key ITR forms for salaried individuals:
- **ITR-1 (Sahaj)**: Single employer salary + one house property + bank interest — simplest
- **ITR-2**: Multiple income sources, capital gains, more than one house property

File through: [www.incometax.gov.in](https://www.incometax.gov.in) — free and takes about 20–30 minutes for simple returns.

## Key Takeaways

1. New regime default from FY 2023-24; no tax up to ₹12.75L (salaried) after 87A rebate
2. Old regime is better if total deductions exceed ~₹3.5L (home loan + 80C + HRA)
3. Always compare both regimes with your actual numbers before choosing
4. Submit Form 12BB early and investment proofs by January 31
5. File your ITR by July 31 — even if TDS is fully paid, filing is mandatory
6. ELSS is the best 80C option if you want market-linked returns with 3-year lock-in
