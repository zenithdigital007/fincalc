# BMI Calculator Guide: Understanding Your Body Mass Index

**BMI (Body Mass Index)** is one of the most widely used tools to assess whether a person's weight is in a healthy range relative to their height. Here's everything you need to know.

## What is BMI?

BMI is a numerical value calculated from your height and weight. It was developed by Belgian mathematician Adolphe Quetelet in the 1830s and remains a standard screening tool used by doctors worldwide.

**Formula:**
BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ Height (m)²

### Example
Weight: 70 kg | Height: 1.72 m (172 cm)
BMI = 70 ÷ (1.72 × 1.72) = 70 ÷ 2.9584 = **23.66**

## BMI Ranges — WHO vs India Standards

The standard WHO BMI classification is widely used globally. However, research shows that **Indians are at higher health risk at lower BMI values** compared to Caucasians, so India-specific cutoffs have been recommended.

### WHO Standard BMI Ranges

| BMI Range | Category |
|-----------|---------|
| Below 18.5 | Underweight |
| 18.5 – 24.9 | Normal weight |
| 25.0 – 29.9 | Overweight |
| 30.0 and above | Obese |

### Recommended BMI Ranges for Indians (ICMR/WHO)

| BMI Range | Category |
|-----------|---------|
| Below 18.0 | Underweight |
| 18.0 – 22.9 | Normal weight |
| 23.0 – 24.9 | Overweight (risk zone) |
| 25.0 – 29.9 | Obese (Class I) |
| 30.0 and above | Obese (Class II) |

This is why Indian doctors often advise maintaining BMI below 23, not just below 25.

## BMI by Gender and Age

BMI interpretation can vary slightly:
- **Children and teens**: Use age- and gender-specific percentile charts (not the adult scale)
- **Elderly (65+)**: Slightly higher BMI (23–27) may actually be protective
- **Athletes**: BMI may be high due to muscle mass, not fat (a limitation of BMI)
- **Pregnant women**: BMI is not used for assessment during pregnancy

## What Your BMI Tells You (and What It Doesn't)

### What BMI tells you
- A general indication of whether your weight is appropriate for your height
- An estimated risk level for weight-related diseases
- A starting point for health discussions with your doctor

### What BMI does NOT tell you
- Where your body fat is distributed (visceral fat vs subcutaneous fat)
- Your muscle mass (a bodybuilder may be "obese" by BMI)
- Your metabolic health (you can have normal BMI but poor metabolic health — "skinny fat")
- Your bone density, fitness level, or diet quality

## Health Risks by BMI Category

| Category | Health Risks |
|---------|-------------|
| Underweight | Malnutrition, anaemia, osteoporosis, immune weakness |
| Normal | Low risk — maintain with diet and exercise |
| Overweight | Increased risk: hypertension, pre-diabetes |
| Obese (Class I) | High risk: Type 2 diabetes, heart disease, sleep apnea |
| Obese (Class II) | Very high risk: stroke, kidney disease, joint problems |

## How to Improve Your BMI

### If you're Overweight or Obese
1. **Diet first** — 500 kcal/day deficit leads to ~0.5 kg/week loss
2. **Increase activity** — 150 min/week moderate exercise (walking, cycling, swimming)
3. **Strength training** — builds muscle, raises metabolism
4. **Track progress** — weigh yourself weekly, not daily
5. **Reduce ultra-processed foods** — biggest driver of weight gain in India

### If you're Underweight
1. **Caloric surplus** — eat 300–500 more kcal than you burn
2. **Protein intake** — 1.2–1.6g/kg bodyweight daily
3. **Strength training** — to gain muscle, not just fat
4. **Rule out medical causes** — thyroid, digestive disorders

## Beyond BMI: Better Metrics

BMI alone is not enough. Use it alongside:

| Metric | Healthy Range (Indians) |
|--------|------------------------|
| Waist circumference | Men: < 90 cm | Women: < 80 cm |
| Waist-to-Hip Ratio | Men: < 0.9 | Women: < 0.85 |
| Body fat % | Men: 8–19% | Women: 21–33% |
| Fasting blood sugar | 70–99 mg/dL |
| Blood pressure | < 120/80 mmHg |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is BMI 25 healthy for Indians?**
A: At BMI 25, most Indians are classified as Obese Class I by ICMR guidelines. Health risks including diabetes and hypertension increase above BMI 23 for South Asians.

**Q: My BMI is normal but I have belly fat — is that okay?**
A: No. Abdominal (visceral) fat is metabolically dangerous even at normal BMI. Check your waist circumference — men should be under 90 cm, women under 80 cm.

**Q: Can BMI be misleading?**
A: Yes. Athletes and bodybuilders often have high BMI due to muscle. Elderly people may lose muscle and have normal BMI despite high fat percentage. Always consult a doctor for a complete assessment.

## Key Takeaways

- BMI = Weight (kg) ÷ Height (m)²
- For Indians, aim for BMI 18.5–22.9 (stricter than WHO guidelines)
- BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnosis
- Combine BMI with waist measurement and metabolic markers for a complete picture
- Small, consistent changes in diet and exercise can significantly improve BMI over 3–6 months
